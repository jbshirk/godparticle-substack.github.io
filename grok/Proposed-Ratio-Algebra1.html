31. Reading path

I would pursue the literature approximately in this order.

Douglas Lundholm & Lars Svensson, Clifford Algebra, Geometric Algebra, and Applications. This gives you tensor algebra, exterior algebra, geometric products, grades and Clifford structure in a unified setting. arXiv

Dan Jonsson, Quantities, Dimensions and Dimensional Analysis. This is directly relevant to your insistence that units/dimensions remain algebraically meaningful rather than disappearing when numerical division is performed. arXiv

Quantity-space/tensor formulations of physical dimensions. Recent work explicitly describes dimensional quantity spaces using tensor products and dual vector spaces, which is conceptually very close to S⊗T∗. arXiv

Burstall et al., quaternionic projective geometry. Especially useful for understanding how a generalized "ratio" of quaternions can be represented by an equivalence class of pairs rather than evaluated. arXiv

Baez, The Octonions. Essential before trying to make the construction octonionic; it explains the exact relationships among division algebras, Clifford algebras, spinors and exceptional structures. arXiv

Škoda, Noncommutative Localization in Noncommutative Geometry. Read this when you reach the question "what could numerator/denominator actually mean in a noncommutative algebra?" arXiv

Desbrun–Hirani–Leok–Marsden, Discrete Exterior Calculus. This becomes highly relevant when you begin constructing the countable/discrete version. arXiv

And Furey's paper is useful as a contrasting example of how the familiar division algebras can be assembled into larger graded structures without claiming that the larger structure is itself simply another member of the normed-division-algebra tower. arXiv
