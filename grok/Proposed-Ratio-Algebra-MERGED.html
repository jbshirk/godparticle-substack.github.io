1. Consensus core (substantially the same in both)

  • both No standard named algebra currently packages the full set of requirements: two mutually orthogonal / dimensionally congruent 3-dimensional aspects S and T, a reciprocal (not additive) relation, numerator & denominator kept as distinguishable algebraic objects, higher grades Sk:Tℓ, possible internal quaternionic/Clifford structure, and (eventually) coefficients that are countable & dense rather than real-continuous.
  • both Do not begin by declaring a new “division algebra.” The classical division-algebra axioms (especially the existence of two-sided inverses that evaluate to scalars) sit in tension with the desire to keep the ratio irreducible.
  • both Provisional working names suggested: bigraded reciprocal algebra, dimensioned ratio algebra, or reciprocal-pair algebra.
  • both Furey’s recent work on the nested sequence ℝ ⊂ ℂ ⊂ ℍ ⊂ 𝕆 ⊂ 𝕍 (and the 15-dimensional vector space 𝕆⊕ℍ⊕ℂ⊕ℝ embedded in 16-dimensional algebras) is useful background but points in a different direction from the irreducible-ratio construction contemplated here.
  • both Correction of the user’s remark on ℚ: the rationals are dense in the reals (not “less dense”). The useful contrast is countable + dense + incomplete versus uncountable + complete. Structures such as ℚ3, quaternion algebras over ℚ, or Clifford algebras over ℚ therefore sit densely inside their real counterparts.
  • both The mathematically natural home for an irreducible quotient of two distinct 3-spaces is the tensor product with the dual: S ⊗ T* ≅ Hom(T, S). This keeps the “numerator” and “denominator” as independently varying objects of different type.
  • both A graded / bigraded exterior-algebra realisation is recommended: objects of reciprocal bidegree (p,q) living in ΛpS ⊗ (ΛqT)*. A first candidate multiplication adds the bidegrees (with the usual exterior truncation when a grade exceeds 3).
  • both Shared literature pointers: quantity calculus / dimensioned algebra (Jonsson, Raposo, Zapata-Carratalá, \ldots), Clifford algebras (especially over orthogonal direct sums), Zorn vector-matrices and the split octonions (because of the suggestive 1+3+3+1 grading), algebras and Clifford algebras defined over ℚ rather than ℝ, and the projective geometry of ratios that remain ratios.

2. Material unique or more developed in one response

  • R1 Explicit three-way disambiguation of the overloaded word “dimension”:
    1. vector-space dimension over ℝ (1,2,4,8 for the division algebras),
    2. grade in an exterior / geometric algebra (0,1,2,3 for a 3-space),
    3. physical / quantity dimension (L, L2, L/T, L3/T3, \ldots).
    The third sense is highlighted as especially relevant to the proposed ratio algebra.
  • R1 Longer, numbered development (roughly 19 sections) that walks through each of the above points in greater detail and ends with a concrete recommended reading order.
  • R1 Explicit global object R(S,T) = ⊕_{p,q=0}^3 V_{p,q} where V_{p,q} = ΛpS ⊗ (ΛqT)*, together with a discussion of possible multiplications, the possible introduction of a metric (turning exterior products into Clifford products), and only afterwards the question of inverses.
  • R2 Sharper initial formalisation of the target object itself: “two dimensionally congruent but algebraically distinct/orthogonal 3-spaces, regarded as reciprocal numerator and denominator aspects, whose quotient is intentionally not evaluated into an element of the underlying scalar field.”
  • R2 Slightly earlier and more compact emphasis on the dual-space / Hom interpretation as the correct model for the irreducible ratio.
  • R2 Overall more concise exposition (approximately 19 k characters versus 26 k).
